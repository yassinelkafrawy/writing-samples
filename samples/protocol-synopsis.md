# A Randomized Phase II Trial of Dexamethasone De-escalation During Elotuzumab–Pomalidomide Therapy in Relapsed or Refractory Multiple Myeloma

**Type** Clinical trial protocol synopsis  
**Intended audience** Clinical investigators, trial operations teams, and ethics reviewers  
**Word count** Approximately 1,000, excluding references and portfolio note  
**Document status** Fictional trial for portfolio purposes only. No study was conducted and no participants were enrolled.

## Rationale

Elotuzumab plus pomalidomide and dexamethasone (EPd) improved progression-free survival compared with pomalidomide and dexamethasone in ELOQUENT-3 [1]. Repeated dexamethasone exposure can be difficult for patients who experience hyperglycemia, insomnia, or mood symptoms. This trial evaluates whether omitting dexamethasone on non-infusion weeks after two cycles of EPd reduces steroid exposure while preserving enough short-term disease control to justify a definitive trial. Both groups retain dexamethasone premedication on elotuzumab infusion days.

## Objectives and endpoints

**Primary objective.** Estimate the between-group difference in disease control at 24 weeks after randomization. The primary endpoint is the proportion of participants alive at week 24 without International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG)–defined progression and without initiation of a new antimyeloma regimen. Death, documented progression, or initiation of a new regimen on or before week 24 constitutes failure [2].

**Secondary objectives.** Characterize progression-free survival, cumulative dexamethasone exposure, safety, and patient-reported symptoms. Secondary endpoints include time from randomization to progression or death, cumulative dexamethasone dose through week 24, grade 3 or higher adverse events, serious infections, infusion reactions, and changes in patient-reported sleep disturbance from randomization to weeks 12 and 24.

## Design

Multicenter, open-label, randomized Phase II screening trial with a two-cycle EPd run-in. At the end of the run-in, participants with stable disease or better who can continue elotuzumab and pomalidomide will be randomized 1:1 through a central system. Randomization will be stratified by age (≤75 versus >75 years) and run-in response (stable disease versus partial response or better). An independent reviewer blinded to treatment assignment will apply IMWG criteria to progression assessments. Run-in attrition and its reasons will be reported separately [2].

## Eligibility

Adults with measurable relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma may enter the run-in if they have received at least two prior lines, including lenalidomide and a proteasome inhibitor, and their treating clinician considers EPd an appropriate next regimen. Additional requirements include an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0–2, an absolute neutrophil count of at least 1.0 × 10⁹/L, a platelet count of at least 75 × 10⁹/L, and creatinine clearance of at least 30 mL/min.

Key exclusions include prior elotuzumab, pomalidomide-refractory disease, active uncontrolled infection, uncontrolled diabetes, a contraindication to the study drugs, and pregnancy or breastfeeding. Participants must comply with applicable pomalidomide pregnancy-prevention requirements. Patients who progress during the run-in leave the treatment phase and may start alternative therapy at their clinician’s discretion.

## Intervention and assessments

During the run-in, both groups receive elotuzumab 10 mg/kg intravenously on days 1, 8, 15, and 22 of each 28-day cycle, pomalidomide 4 mg orally on days 1–21, and age-adjusted dexamethasone with infusion premedication. From cycle 3 onward, both groups receive elotuzumab 20 mg/kg intravenously on day 1 and continue pomalidomide on days 1–21 [3].

- **Control group.** Continue age-adjusted dexamethasone on infusion day and oral dexamethasone on days 8, 15, and 22. The oral dose on non-infusion days is 40 mg for participants aged 75 years or younger and 20 mg for those older than 75 [3].
- **De-escalation group.** Continue the same age-adjusted dexamethasone and other premedications on infusion day. Omit scheduled dexamethasone on days 8, 15, and 22.

Treatment continues until progression, unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal, or investigator decision. The protocol permits clinically indicated dose holds, reductions, and rescue corticosteroids. Sites will record their dates, doses, and reasons. Disease assessments occur at baseline, at randomization, and every four weeks through week 24, then every eight weeks through month 12 while the participant remains progression-free and has agreed to follow-up.

## Safety monitoring and trial conduct

Sites will monitor blood counts, renal function, glucose, infections, thromboembolic events, and infusion reactions. The protocol will specify criteria for treatment interruption and resumption, risk-based thromboprophylaxis, and management of infusion reactions. Investigators will report serious adverse events to the sponsor within 24 hours of awareness, as specified in the protocol. The sponsor will assess expedited reporting obligations. An independent safety committee will review deaths, serious infections, severe infusion reactions, and early progression after the first 20 randomized participants complete eight weeks, with authority to recommend a pause or amendment [3,4].

Written informed consent is required before trial-specific procedures, following ethics committee approval and applicable regulatory authorization. Consent materials will explain the experimental schedule, alternatives, foreseeable risks, and the right to stop treatment or decline further follow-up. The sponsor will prioritize consent documentation, eligibility, treatment assignment, dosing, progression assessments, and serious adverse-event reporting in its risk-based monitoring plan. Study records will use coded identifiers, role-based access, and auditable corrections [4].

## Statistical analysis

The trial will randomize 80 participants, 40 per group. The sample size supports estimation in a screening trial. With a 70% week-24 disease-control rate in each group, the approximate 95% confidence interval for the between-group difference would have a half-width of 20 percentage points. A formal noninferiority conclusion would require a larger trial and a prespecified, clinically justified margin.

The primary analysis will include all randomized participants in their assigned groups and report the absolute risk difference with a two-sided 95% confidence interval. Participants who discontinue assigned therapy will remain in follow-up where consent permits. Participants whose week-24 outcome cannot be established will count as failures in the primary analysis. A sensitivity analysis will vary the assumed outcomes for missing assessments by group. Safety summaries will include all participants who receive at least one dose after randomization, grouped by treatment received, with run-in safety reported separately. The decision to advance the strategy will consider the estimated loss of disease control and its uncertainty alongside steroid exposure, symptom burden, and serious adverse events.
